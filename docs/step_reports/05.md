## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

### 1.1 Objective
The core tenet of Step 5 is **"Avoid overstating; show how sure the system is."**  
Prior to this step, Nivara's machine learning models and analytical routines generated point forecasts (e.g., "Expected load: 24.5 kW") and crisp savings estimates (e.g., "Saves ₹14,200/mo") without communicating underlying statistical variance, estimation uncertainty, or detector confidence. In mission-critical campus energy management, point estimates can lead to false precision, eroded operator trust, or premature capital commitments.

Step 5 transforms Nivara from a deterministic reporting tool into a calibrated, uncertainty-aware decision-support platform across four pillars:

1. **Parametric & Empirical Forecast Prediction Intervals:**
   - Augmented `ExpectedConsumptionModel` (Ridge regression) with empirical residual quantile offsets ($10^{\text{th}}$ and $90^{\text{th}}$ percentiles), yielding calibrated nominal 80% prediction intervals ($[\hat{y}_{\text{lower}}, \hat{y}_{\text{upper}}]$) that enforce physical non-negativity ($\hat{y}_{\text{lower}} \ge 0$).
   - Standardized Meta Prophet native uncertainty extraction ($80\%$ interval) and guaranteed monotonic bounds across all forecast horizons.
   - Implemented what-if scenario uncertainty propagation, producing synchronized baseline and counterfactual ribbons along with three-point savings ranges $\{\text{low}, \text{expected}, \text{high}\}$.

2. **Empirical Prediction Interval Coverage Benchmarking:**
   - Integrated interval coverage calculation ($\% \text{ of actual test observations } \in [\hat{y}_{\text{lower}}, \hat{y}_{\text{upper}}]$) into `forecast_eval.py` and `run_all.py`.
   - On the 7-day held-out test set (2,016 timesteps), **Ridge achieved 81.4% empirical coverage** and **Prophet achieved 82.2% empirical coverage**, closely matching the 80% nominal target.

3. **Formal Anomaly Confidence & Impact-Based Severity:**
   - Established a documented, bounded confidence scoring formula:
     $$\text{confidence} = \min\left(1.0, \max\left(0.10, \text{base\_confidence} + \text{layer\_agreement\_bonus} + \text{residual\_magnitude\_score}\right)\right)$$
     with explicit detector base weights (Physical Rule: 0.85, Statistical Ridge: 0.65, Isolation Forest: 0.50), a multi-layer agreement bonus ($+0.15$), and normalized residual magnitude scaling.
   - Enforced physical cost/impact-based severity categorization (`CRITICAL`, `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `LOW`) grounded in the system assumption registry.

4. **Standardized Three-Point Savings Ranges & Disclaimers:**
   - Refactored savings calculations across ESG reporting, work order prioritization, forecasting scenarios, and executive KPIs to strictly output $\{\text{low}, \text{expected}, \text{high}\}$ triplets for energy (kWh), financial impact (₹ INR), and carbon abatement ($\text{kgCO}_2\text{e}$).
   - Deployed the standardized decision-support disclaimer banner across all UI touchpoints:
     `"⚠️ Decision-support estimate, not an official measurement."`

5. **Visual Uncertainty Interface & Complete Test Suite:**
   - Upgraded Streamlit Page 4 (AI Forecasting) with shaded Plotly confidence bands (`fill='tonexty'`), interval coverage KPI cards, and scenario range levers.
   - Upgraded Page 5 (Alerts) with confidence progress bars, multi-layer consensus badges, and relative deviation metrics.
   - Created `tests/test_uncertainty.py` with 8 comprehensive unit tests, expanding the platform test suite to **97 passing tests** with zero regressions.

## 3. Mathematical & Algorithmic Formulations

### 3.1 Empirical Residual Quantile Prediction Intervals (Ridge Regression)

Standard linear and ridge regressions generate conditional expectation estimates $\mathbb{E}[Y | X = x] = \hat{y}(x)$, but fail to represent prediction variance. Rather than relying on rigid Gaussian error assumptions (which fail due to asymmetric building load distributions), Nivara adopts a **non-parametric empirical residual quantile approach**:

1. **Residual Computation on Training Set:**  
   Given training observations $(x_i, y_i)_{i=1}^N$ and fitted model $\hat{f}(x)$, in-sample residuals are calculated as:
   $$e_i = y_i - \hat{f}(x_i) \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$$

2. **Quantile Offsets for Nominal $100(1 - \alpha)\%$ Interval:**  
   For a nominal $80\%$ prediction interval ($\alpha = 0.20$), empirical quantiles are computed at percentiles $q_{\text{low}} = 100 \times \frac{\alpha}{2} = 10\%$ and $q_{\text{high}} = 100 \times (1 - \frac{\alpha}{2}) = 90\%$:
   $$\delta_{\text{lower}} = \text{Percentile}_{10}(\{e_1, \dots, e_N\})$$
   $$\delta_{\text{upper}} = \text{Percentile}_{90}(\{e_1, \dots, e_N\})$$

3. **Interval Generation with Physical Constraints:**  
   For any future feature vector $x^*$, the point forecast $\hat{y} = \hat{f}(x^*)$ is bounded by:
   $$\hat{y}_{\text{lower}} = \max\left(0.0, \, \min(\hat{y}, \, \hat{y} + \delta_{\text{lower}})\right)$$
   $$\hat{y}_{\text{upper}} = \max(\hat{y}, \, \hat{y} + \delta_{\text{upper}})$$
   This guarantees that:
   $$0 \le \hat{y}_{\text{lower}} \le \hat{y} \le \hat{y}_{\text{upper}}$$

4. **Scenario Propagation:**  
   When a what-if scenario applies an operational scaling multiplier $m = (1 - \frac{\Delta\%}{100})$, the uncertainty bands scale proportionally:
   $$\hat{y}_{\text{scenario, lower}} = \hat{y}_{\text{lower}} \times m$$
   $$\hat{y}_{\text{scenario, upper}} = \hat{y}_{\text{upper}} \times m$$
   The resulting energy savings range over $H$ forecast periods is:
   $$\text{Savings}_{\text{low}} = \sum_{t=1}^H \max\left(0.0, \, \hat{y}_{\text{baseline, lower}, t} - \hat{y}_{\text{scenario, upper}, t}\right)$$
   $$\text{Savings}_{\text{expected}} = \sum_{t=1}^H \max\left(0.0, \, \hat{y}_{\text{baseline}, t} - \hat{y}_{\text{scenario}, t}\right)$$
   $$\text{Savings}_{\text{high}} = \sum_{t=1}^H \max\left(0.0, \, \hat{y}_{\text{baseline, upper}, t} - \hat{y}_{\text{scenario, lower}, t}\right)$$
   guaranteeing $\text{Savings}_{\text{low}} \le \text{Savings}_{\text{expected}} \le \text{Savings}_{\text{high}}$.

### 3.2 Prophet Native Uncertainty Mapping

Meta Prophet computes Bayesian posterior predictive intervals by default via Monte Carlo simulations of the trend and additive seasonal components:
- Nominal interval width: $\text{interval\_width} = 0.80$ (80% prediction interval).
- Extracted series: `yhat_lower`, `yhat`, `yhat_upper`.
- Post-processing safety clamps:
  $$\hat{y}_{\text{lower}} = \max\left(0.0, \, \min(\hat{y}, \, \hat{y}_{\text{prophet, lower}})\right)$$
  $$\hat{y}_{\text{upper}} = \max\left(\hat{y}, \, \hat{y}_{\text{prophet, upper}}\right)$$

### 3.3 Formal Anomaly Confidence Scoring Formulation

Prior alerts reported generic severity without indicating detector confidence or multi-layer agreement. The new calibrated formulation is:

$$\text{Confidence} = \min\left(1.0, \, \max\left(0.10, \, C_{\text{base}} + C_{\text{agreement}} + C_{\text{residual}}\right)\right)$$

Where:
1. **Base Detector Confidence ($C_{\text{base}}$):**
   - **Physical Rule Layer** (`unoccupied_operation`, `pump_no_flow`, `sensor_dropout`): $C_{\text{base}} = 0.85$  
     *(High deterministic confidence because violation relies on incontrovertible physical domain facts like scheduled vacancy or zero hydraulic flow).*
   - **Statistical Regression Layer** (`high_consumption`): $C_{\text{base}} = 0.65$  
     *(Moderate confidence; reflects baseline model prediction error).*
   - **Isolation Forest ML Layer** (`unusual_pattern`): $C_{\text{base}} = 0.50$  
     *(Exploratory non-parametric confidence; sensitive to high-dimensional sensor noise).*

2. **Multi-Layer Agreement Bonus ($C_{\text{agreement}}$):**
   $$C_{\text{agreement}} = \begin{cases} +0.15 & \text{if } \ge 2 \text{ distinct detection layers flagged this asset within } \pm 60 \text{ min} \\ 0.00 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

3. **Residual Magnitude Score ($C_{\text{residual}}$):**  
   Scaled by the relative magnitude of the excess consumption over the expected baseline:
   $$C_{\text{residual}} = \min\left(0.20, \, \frac{\Delta\text{kW}}{\max(1.0, \text{baseline\_kw})} \times 0.20\right)$$

4. **Range Guarantee:**  
   $\text{Confidence} \in [0.10, 1.00]$.

### 3.4 Cost & Impact-Based Anomaly Severity

Alert severity is classified into four operational tiers based on equipment criticality, thermal risk, and excess power:

| Severity | Definition & Trigger Criteria | Action Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| **CRITICAL** | Statistical excess $\Delta\text{kW} \ge 2.0\text{ kW}$ (assumptions registry threshold), dry-running utility water pumps (`pump_no_flow`), or major sensor dropout on key substations. | Immediate maintenance dispatch; automatic escalation to Facilities Director. |
| **HIGH** | After-hours HVAC or pump operation during unoccupied periods ($\Delta\text{kW} \ge 1.0\text{ kW}$), or prolonged statistical deviation ($\Delta\text{kW} \ge 1.5\text{ kW}$). | Priority work order generation within 2 hours; scheduled resolution before next daybreak. |
| **MEDIUM** | After-hours lighting in classrooms/labs, moderate statistical excess ($0.5 \le \Delta\text{kW} < 1.5\text{ kW}$), or sustained sensor flutter. | Operator review during standard morning shift sweep. |
| **LOW** | Minor sensor dropout, low-draw equipment deviation ($< 0.5\text{ kW}$), or single-timestep isolation forest flag. | Passive logging in telemetry audit ledger. |

### 3.5 Standardized Three-Point Savings Ranges

All energy waste and cost reduction estimates must account for tariff tier variability and baseline uncertainty:

$$\text{Waste}_{\text{daily}} = \text{excess\_kw} \times \text{hours\_per\_day}$$
$$\text{Cost}_{\text{expected}} = \text{Waste}_{\text{daily}} \times \text{Tariff}_{\text{blended}} \quad (\text{₹}10.75/\text{kWh})$$
$$\text{Cost}_{\text{low}} = 0.70 \times \text{Cost}_{\text{expected}}$$
$$\text{Cost}_{\text{high}} = 1.30 \times \text{Cost}_{\text{expected}}$$
$$\text{Carbon}_{\{\text{low}, \text{expected}, \text{high}\}} = \text{Waste}_{\{\text{low}, \text{expected}, \text{high}\}} \times \text{GridFactor} \quad (0.716\text{ kgCO}_2\text{e/kWh})$$

Standard UI Representation:
- Currency: `Expected: ₹14,200 (Range: ₹9,940 – ₹18,460)`
- Energy: `1,320 kWh [924 – 1,716 kWh]`
- Mandatory Caption: `⚠️ Decision-support estimate, not an official measurement.`

## 7. Known Limitations & Scientific Assumptions

1. **Stationarity of In-Sample Residuals:**  
   The empirical quantile interval method assumes that the distribution of residuals in the training set reflects future error variance. If extreme ambient weather shocks or unplanned campus shutdowns occur, empirical coverage may temporarily dip below nominal.
2. **Homoscedastic Offset vs. Heteroscedastic Variance:**  
   The current Ridge regression implementation applies constant scalar quantile offsets ($\delta_{\text{lower}}, \delta_{\text{upper}}$) across all hours of the day. While computationally fast and robust on 80% coverage (achieving 81.4%), future work in Step 6 could implement hour-of-day quantile binning to capture diurnal variance heteroscedasticity.
3. **Synthetic Ground Truth Extrapolation:**  
   As documented in the platform disclaimer, baseline coverage metrics are validated on 28 days of synthetic campus telemetry with injected faults. Real-world sensor dropout, network delays, and meter drift require ongoing online calibration.
