## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

### 1.1 Objective
The central objective of Step 9 is to **make Nivara's recommendations reflect how Indian commercial, educational, and healthcare facilities actually pay for electric power.**

Prior to Step 9, energy analytics in Nivara calculated operating expenditures primarily on a flat, single-tier electricity tariff ($\approx ₹8.50/\text{kWh}$). While suitable for preliminary energy benchmarking, Indian institutional consumers connected at High Tension (HT, 11 kV or 33 kV) operate under complex statutory tariff structures governed by State Electricity Regulatory Commissions (such as the **Odisha Electricity Regulatory Commission - OERC** and distribution licensees like **TP Central Odisha Distribution Limited - TPCODL**).

Under Indian HT utility regulations:
1. **Fixed Demand Charges (kVA):** Facilities pay substantial monthly fixed charges ($\approx ₹250/\text{kVA/month}$) based on **Billing Demand**, defined as $\max(\text{Recorded Peak kVA}, 80\% \times \text{Contract Demand})$. Exceeding sanctioned contract demand incurs heavy surcharge penalties.
2. **Power Factor (PF) Surcharges & Incentives:** Facilities maintaining an average monthly $\text{PF} < 0.90$ are penalized with a direct surcharge of **1.0% on total energy charges per 0.01 drop below 0.90**. Conversely, facilities maintaining $\text{PF} > 0.95$ receive an incentive rebate of **0.5% per 0.01 rise above 0.95**.
3. **Time-of-Day (ToD) Pricing Bands:** Energy consumed during the evening peak window (18:00–22:00) carries a 20% premium ($₹10.20/\text{kWh}$), whereas night off-peak energy (00:00–06:00) offers a 15% discount ($₹7.225/\text{kWh}$), opening a ₹2.975/kWh arbitrage spread.
4. **Standby Diesel Generation (DG):** Power outages or unoptimized peak runs rely on diesel generator sets where fuel costs ($\approx ₹95/\text{L}$, specific fuel consumption $\approx 0.30\,\text{L/kWh}$) lead to an effective generation cost of $\approx ₹28.50/\text{kWh}$—over $3.3\times$ higher than grid power, with high carbon intensity ($\approx 2.68\,\text{kg CO}_2\text{e/L}$).

Step 9 establishes native support for these institutional realities across the tariff configuration, the decision engine, safety enforcement, and the interactive dashboard.

## 3. Mathematical Formulations & Tariff Modeling

### 3.1 Maximum Demand (kVA) & Apparent Power

Utility meters compute instantaneous apparent power (kVA) from active power (kW) and displacement power factor (PF):

$$\text{kVA} = \frac{\text{kW}}{\max(0.10, \text{PF})}$$

For three-phase unbalanced feeder telemetry with line voltage $V_L$ and line current $I_L$:

$$\text{kVA} = \frac{\sqrt{3} \cdot V_L \cdot I_L}{1000}$$

#### Two-Part Tariff Billing Demand Formula
Per OERC HT Commercial regulations, monthly Billing Demand ($BD$) is calculated as:

$$BD = \max\left(\text{Recorded Peak kVA}, \quad \text{Contract Demand} \times \frac{\text{Billing Demand \%}}{100}\right)$$

Where $\text{Billing Demand \%} = 80\%$.
- If $\text{Recorded Peak kVA} < 0.80 \times CD$, the facility is billed for $0.80 \times CD$.
- If $\text{Recorded Peak kVA} \ge 0.80 \times CD$, the facility is billed for actual peak kVA.
- If $\text{Recorded Peak kVA} > CD$, the consumer violates sanctioned demand, triggering penal surcharges.

Monthly demand charges:

$$\text{Demand Charges (INR)} = BD \times \text{Rate}_{\text{kVA}} \quad (\text{Rate}_{\text{kVA}} = ₹250.0/\text{kVA/month})$$

#### Peak Demand Shaving Savings
Reducing peak demand by $\Delta\text{kVA}$ through BESS dispatch or load staggering yields:

$$\Delta BD = BD_{\text{recorded}} - \max(\text{Recorded Peak} - \Delta\text{kVA}, 0.80 \times CD)$$
$$\text{Annual Demand Savings (INR)} = \Delta BD \times 250 \times 12$$

BESS sizing for a 2-hour shaving window at 85% depth of discharge:

$$\text{BESS Capacity (kWh)} = \frac{\Delta\text{kVA} \times 2.0}{0.85}$$
$$\text{Capex (INR)} = \text{BESS Capacity} \times ₹18,000/\text{kWh}$$

### 3.2 Power Factor (PF) Surcharge & Incentive Formulations

Per OERC / TPCODL tariff orders:
- **Baseline / Penalty Threshold:** $\text{PF}_{\text{penalty}} = 0.90$
- **Incentive Threshold:** $\text{PF}_{\text{incentive}} = 0.95$
- **Target APFC Operation:** $\text{PF}_{\text{target}} = 0.98$

#### Penalty Surcharge Formula
For an average monthly power factor $\text{PF}_{\text{avg}} < 0.90$:

$$\text{Drop Points} = \text{round}\left((0.90 - \text{PF}_{\text{avg}}) \times 100, 2\right)$$
$$\text{Surcharge Fraction} = \text{Drop Points} \times 1.0\% = (0.90 - \text{PF}_{\text{avg}})$$
$$\text{Monthly Penalty (INR)} = \text{Monthly Energy Charges} \times \text{Surcharge Fraction}$$

*Example:* An institution with $\text{PF}_{\text{avg}} = 0.84$ incurs a drop of $0.06$ (6 points), resulting in a **6.0% surcharge** on its entire monthly energy bill.

#### Incentive Rebate Formula
For an average monthly power factor $\text{PF}_{\text{avg}} > 0.95$:

$$\text{Rise Points} = \text{round}\left((\text{PF}_{\text{avg}} - 0.95) \times 100, 2\right)$$
$$\text{Incentive Fraction} = \text{Rise Points} \times 0.5\% = (\text{PF}_{\text{avg}} - 0.95) \times 0.5$$
$$\text{Monthly Incentive Rebate (INR)} = \text{Monthly Energy Charges} \times \text{Incentive Fraction}$$

*Example:* Correcting PF to $0.98$ produces a rise of $0.03$ (3 points), earning a **1.5% rebate** on monthly energy charges.

#### APFC Capacitor Sizing Formulation
To shift operating power factor from $\text{PF}_1$ to $\text{PF}_2$ for average active load $P$ (kW):

$$\tan(\phi_1) = \frac{\sqrt{1 - \text{PF}_1^2}}{\text{PF}_1}, \qquad \tan(\phi_2) = \frac{\sqrt{1 - \text{PF}_2^2}}{\text{PF}_2}$$
$$\text{kVAR}_{\text{required}} = P \cdot \left(\tan(\phi_1) - \tan(\phi_2)\right)$$

Standard commercial sizing rounds up to the nearest 5 kVAR step.
Turnkey APFC capex:

$$\text{Capex}_{\text{APFC}} = \text{kVAR}_{\text{required}} \times ₹1,200/\text{kVAR} \quad (\text{Assumptions: } \texttt{capital\_cost.apfc\_per\_kvar})$$

### 3.3 Time-of-Day (ToD) Tariff Load Shifting

The OERC HT Commercial tariff establishes three distinct pricing bands:

$$\text{Rate}(h) = \begin{cases}
₹10.20/\text{kWh} & \text{if } 18 \le h < 22 \quad (\text{Peak Band, } +20\%) \\
₹7.225/\text{kWh} & \text{if } 0 \le h < 6 \quad (\text{Off-Peak Band, } -15\%) \\
₹8.50/\text{kWh} & \text{otherwise} \quad (\text{Normal Band, } 1.0\times)
\end{cases}$$

$$\Delta\text{Rate} = \text{Rate}_{\text{peak}} - \text{Rate}_{\text{off\_peak}} = 10.20 - 7.225 = ₹2.975/\text{kWh}$$

For deferrable loads (such as water transfer pumps `EQ-UTL-P1-PM`, batch laundry, or thermal water heaters) consuming $E_{\text{peak}}$ kWh during the 18:00–22:00 window:

$$\text{Annual Arbitrage Savings (INR)} = E_{\text{peak, daily}} \times 365 \times ₹2.975/\text{kWh}$$

Implementation capex is ₹0 (operational schedule shift via BMS or digital timer relay).

### 3.4 Standby Diesel Generator (DG) Economics

For facilities equipped with captive diesel generator sets (e.g. `EQ-UTL-H-GEN-DG`):
- High-Speed Diesel Procurement Cost: $C_{\text{fuel}} = ₹95.0/\text{L}$ (`tariff.diesel_inr_per_l`)
- Direct Combustion GHG Emission Factor: $E_{\text{diesel}} = 2.68\,\text{kg CO}_2\text{e/L}$ (`emissions.diesel_kg_per_l`)
- Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC): $\text{SFC} = 0.30\,\text{L/kWh}$ (`dg.specific_fuel_consumption_l_per_kwh`)

$$\text{DG Generation Cost} = 0.30\,\text{L/kWh} \times ₹95.0/\text{L} = ₹28.50/\text{kWh}$$

$$\text{Cost Multiplier vs Grid} = \frac{₹28.50}{₹8.50} \approx 3.35\times$$

$$\text{DG Direct Emissions} = 0.30 \times 2.68 = 0.804\,\text{kg CO}_2\text{e/kWh}$$

*Roadmap Status:* Real-time Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) logging, ultrasonic diesel fuel tank telemetry, and multi-source microgrid dispatch (Solar + BESS + DG) are scheduled on the Nivara development roadmap.

## 4. Service-Impact Guard Integration

A critical invariant of the Nivara architecture is that energy conservation or peak demand shaving measures **must never jeopardize life-critical clinical services, cold-chain storage, or academic research integrity.**

Under Step 9:
1. `_is_curtailment_action` in `src/campus_energy/decision/guard.py` was extended to include `"peak_demand_shaving"` and `"tod_load_shifting"`.
2. Any peak shaving or load shifting action whose target matches a non-curtailable protected load (e.g., `EQ-IPD-ICU-EQ` in District Hospital, `EQ-A-CL01-PC` examination servers in University, or vaccine refrigeration units) is **immediately intercepted and blocked** by the Service-Impact Guard with `status="blocked"`.
3. In `build_ranked_recommendations`, blocked actions are isolated exclusively into `blocked_recommendations` and strictly forbidden from appearing in the approved action queue.
